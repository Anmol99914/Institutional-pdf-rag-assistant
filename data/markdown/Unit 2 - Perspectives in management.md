Introduction to Management Perspectives 
Management did not develop overnight. It evolved over time as organizations faced new 
challenges during industrialization, globalization, and technological advancement. 
Management scholars developed different theories and perspectives to improve 
organizational efficiency and productivity.

shaped modern management practices. Their theories helped organizations improve 
efficiency, structure, and productivity.

1. Classical Perspective of Management 
The classical perspective emerged during the late 19th and early 20th centuries when 
industries were growing rapidly. 
The main objective of classical management was to increase productivity and efficiency in 
organizations. 
This perspective includes three major theories: 
1. Scientific Management
 
2. Administrative Management
 
3. Bureaucratic Management

1.1 Scientific Management (Frederick W. 
Taylor) 
Frederick Taylor is known as the Father of Scientific Management. 
He believed that productivity could be improved by applying scientific methods to work 
processes. 
Principles of Scientific Management 
Taylor proposed the following principles: 
1. Science, not rule of thumb

 Work should be performed using scientific methods rather than traditional practices.
 
2. Scientific selection and training of workers

 Employees should be carefully selected and trained for their tasks.
 
3. Cooperation between management and workers

 Managers and workers should work together to improve productivity.
 
4. Equal division of work and responsibility

 Management plans work, while workers perform tasks.
 
Example 
In a manufacturing company, Taylor suggested studying worker movements and task timing 
to determine the most efficient way of performing a task. 
Similarly, in a software company, productivity can be improved by: 
● optimizing coding practices
 
● improving development workflows

# Page 10

Advantages / Contributions 
1. Improved productivity
 
Scientific management helped organizations increase worker productivity by identifying the most 
efficient methods of performing tasks. 
Example: 
Time-and-motion studies reduced unnecessary movements during production. 
 
2. Standardization of work
 
Work processes became standardized, making it easier to train workers and maintain quality. 
Example: 
Standard coding guidelines in software development teams. 
 
3. Scientific selection and training
 
Workers are selected and trained based on skills and suitability for tasks. 
 
4. Better cooperation between workers and management
 
Taylor emphasized collaboration between managers and workers to improve efficiency. 
 
5. Foundation for modern industrial management
 
Many modern management techniques such as work measurement and performance 
management originated from scientific management.

# Page 11

Limitations / Criticisms 
1. Ignores human factors
 
The theory focuses mainly on productivity and efficiency, neglecting worker emotions and social 
needs. 
 
2. Workers treated like machines
 
Employees are seen as tools for production rather than individuals with psychological needs. 
 
3. Lack of flexibility
 
Standardized work procedures may not work effectively in complex or creative environments. 
Example: 
Software development often requires creativity rather than rigid procedures. 
 
4. Resistance from workers
 
Workers sometimes resist strict control and repetitive work methods.

2. Behavioral Perspective 
The behavioral perspective focuses on human behavior and motivation in organizations. 
Earlier management theories emphasized efficiency and productivity, but behavioral theorists 
recognized that employees’ psychological and social needs are also important. 
Earlier theories ignored human emotions, motivation, and social behavior. 
The behavioral perspective emerged to answer this gap. 
Core Idea: Employees are not just machines — they are human beings with emotions, needs, and 
social relationships.

1. Hawthorne Studies (Most Important)

○ Elton Mayo

2.1. Hawthorne Studies (Most Important) 
Background 
● Conducted at Western Electric Company, Hawthorne Plant (USA)
 
● Between 1924 – 1932
 
● Led by Elton Mayo and his team
 
Objective 
To study how working conditions affect worker productivity

# Page 18

Stages of Hawthorne Experiments 
1. Illumination Experiment 
Purpose 
To study the effect of lighting (illumination) on worker productivity. 
Process 
● Workers were divided into two groups:
 
○ one group with increased lighting
 
○ another group with normal lighting
 
Result 
Productivity increased in both groups, even when lighting was reduced. 
Conclusion 
Lighting was not the main factor affecting productivity. The “team” feeling contributed to the 
increase in productivity. 
 
2. Relay Assembly Test Room Experiment 
Purpose 
To study the effect of: 
● working hours
 
● rest pauses
 
● wages
 
● supervision

# Page 19

on productivity. 
Process 
● A small group of workers was selected
 
● Working conditions were changed (rest breaks, incentives, etc.)
 
Result 
Productivity consistently increased, even when conditions were changed back. 
Conclusion 
Workers performed better because: 
● they felt important
 
● they received special attention
 
● they worked in a friendly environment
 
 
3. Interviewing Programme 
Purpose 
To understand the attitudes, feelings, and opinions of workers regarding their job, 
supervision, and working conditions. 
 
Process 
● A large number of employees were interviewed.
 
● Workers were encouraged to express their views freely.
 
● Interviews shifted from structured questions to non-directive discussions.

# Page 20

Findings 
● Worker behavior is influenced by emotions, attitudes, and social factors.
 
● Employees want to be heard and understood.
 
● Job satisfaction depends on:
 
○ supervision
 
○ recognition
 
○ relationships with co-workers
 
● Communication between management and workers improves performance.
 
 
Conclusion 
Productivity is affected not only by physical conditions but also by psychological and social 
factors. 
 
 
4. Bank Wiring Observation Room Experiment 
Purpose 
To study the impact of group behavior and social relationships on worker productivity. 
 
Process 
● A group of workers (wiring operators) was placed in a separate room.
 
● Their work and interactions were observed without changing physical conditions.

# Page 21

● The group was paid based on output (incentive system).
 
 
Findings 
● Workers did not increase production even when incentives were offered.
 
● Instead, they intentionally restricted output.
 
● Informal group norms developed, where:
 
○ workers agreed on a standard level of output
 
○ anyone exceeding it was discouraged or pressured
 
● Workers feared that:
 
○ high output may lead to higher targets in the future
 
○ or job loss for others
 
 
Conclusion 
● Informal groups strongly influence individual behavior.
 
● Social pressure can be more powerful than financial incentives.
 
● Workers behave according to group norms, not just individual motivation.

Key Findings of Hawthorne Studies 
1. Social factors are more important than physical conditions
 
2. Employees are motivated by recognition and attention
 
3. Informal groups influence behavior
 
4. Communication improves performance
 
5. Leadership style affects productivity

Advantages of Behavioral Perspective 
1. Focus on human needs: Recognizes that employees have emotions, needs, and 
motivations. 
2. Improves employee motivation: Managers use incentives, recognition, and 
participation. 
3. Better communication: Encourages open communication between employees and 
managers. 
4. Improved teamwork: Emphasizes group work and cooperation. 
5. Better work environment: Leads to higher job satisfaction and reduced conflict. 
6. Foundation for HR management: Modern HR practices (training, motivation, 
leadership) are based on this approach.

# Page 25

Limitations of Behavioral Perspective 
1. Overemphasis on human relations: May ignore productivity and efficiency. 
2. Difficult to measure behavior: Human behavior is complex and unpredictable. 
3. Not universally applicable: Different people respond differently to motivation 
techniques. 
4. May reduce discipline: Too much focus on employee satisfaction may reduce control. 
5. Time-consuming: Building relationships and motivation takes time. 
 
3. Quantitative Perspective 
The quantitative perspective is a broad approach that emphasizes the use of quantitative 
techniques, mathematical models, and statistical analysis to improve managerial 
decision-making. 
It focuses on applying numerical methods to analyze problems and support decisions in 
organizations. 
It developed mainly during World War II, when mathematical models were used to solve 
complex operational problems. 
The quantitative perspective is mainly divided into: 
1. Management Science
 
2. Operations Management
 
 
3.1. Management Science 
Management science is a specialized area within the quantitative perspective that focuses on 
developing mathematical models and analytical methods to solve specific managerial 
problems. 
It is more concerned with problem-solving techniques and model building.

# Page 26

Techniques Used 
● Linear programming
 
● Queuing theory
 
● Simulation models
 
● Decision theory
 
● Forecasting
 
 
Example 
A company uses mathematical models to: 
● determine optimal production levels
 
● minimize costs
 
● maximize profit
 
In IT companies, algorithms and data models are used to optimize system performance and 
resource allocation.

Operations management focuses on the planning, organizing, and controlling of production

1. Improves decision making: Managers can make decisions based on data rather than

2. Efficient resource allocation: Mathematical models help optimize resources. 
Example: 
● inventory control
 
● production scheduling
 
3. Useful in complex situations: Quantitative techniques help analyze complex business

Limitations / Criticisms 
1. Ignores human factors: Quantitative methods often overlook human behavior and 
motivation. 
2. Complex calculations: These techniques may require advanced mathematical skills. 
3. Dependence on accurate data: If the data used in models is incorrect, decisions may 
be misleading. 
 
4. Integrating Perspectives 
The integrating perspective combines the ideas of earlier management theories to provide a 
more complete and practical approach to management. 
No single theory is sufficient to explain all management situations. Therefore, managers need to 
integrate different perspectives. 
Need for Integration 
Each management theory has its own focus: 
● Classical → efficiency and structure
 
● Behavioral → human relations
 
● Quantitative → mathematical analysis
 
However: 
● Classical theory ignores human behavior
 
● Behavioral theory ignores efficiency
 
● Quantitative theory ignores human factors
 
Therefore, an integrated approach is needed.

# Page 29

The integration of management theories is mainly achieved through: 
1. Systems Approach
 
2. Contingency Approach 
 
4.1 Systems Approach 
The systems approach views an organization as a set of interrelated and interdependent 
parts working together to achieve a common goal. 
It treats the organization as a whole system rather than separate units. 
Components of a System

# Page 30

Example 
In a software company: 
● Inputs → developers, tools, data
 
● Process → coding, testing
 
● Output → software product
 
● Feedback → user reviews 
 
Advantages / Contributions 
1. Holistic view of organization: The theory considers the organization as a whole rather 
than focusing on individual parts. 
2. Improves coordination: Different departments work together effectively. 
3. Encourages integration: The approach integrates various organizational activities. 
4. Adaptability: Organizations can respond better to environmental changes. 
 
Limitations / Criticisms 
1. Abstract concept: The systems approach may be difficult to apply in practice. 
2. Complex analysis: Understanding the interaction of all components can be challenging. 
3. Lack of specific managerial guidelines: The theory explains relationships but does not 
provide clear management rules.
 
 
4.2 Contingency Approach 
The contingency approach states that: 
There is no single best way to manage an organization. 
Management practices should depend on the specific situation or environment.

# Page 31

Managers must adjust their strategies based on: 
● Organizational size,
 
● Task technology,
 
● Environmental Uncertainty, 
● Individual differences, 
● The geographical spread of the organization, and 
● The type of work being done. 
Example 
A startup company may use flexible management practices, while a large corporation may 
require more structured management systems. 
 
Advantages / Contributions 
1. Flexible management approach: Managers can adapt strategies based on 
circumstances. 
2. Realistic approach: Recognizes that organizations operate in dynamic environments. 
3. Encourages situational analysis: Managers consider factors such as: 
● environment 
● technology 
● Workforce 
 
Limitations / Criticisms 
1. Difficult to apply: Managers may struggle to determine the best approach for each 
situation. 
2. Lack of clear guidelines: The theory does not provide specific management principles. 
3. Requires experienced managers: Managers must have strong analytical skills to apply 
contingency thinking.
